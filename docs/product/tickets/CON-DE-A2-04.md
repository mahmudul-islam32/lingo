## User story
As a learner, I want to invite people, congratulate them and say who I give presents to, so
that I can take part in birthdays, weddings and office celebrations.

## Unit outline
- **Theme:** invitations, birthdays, weddings, presents, liking things
- **Life in DACH:** the custom of bringing your own cake to the office on your birthday;
  invitations with a reply
- **Can-do:** I can invite someone and accept or decline, congratulate people, say who I
  give a present to, and say whether I like something.
- **Grammar:** **dative case**: `dem / der / dem / den …-n`, `einem / einer`, `meinem /
  meiner`; indirect object with `geben`, `schenken`, `zeigen`; **dative pronouns** `mir`,
  `dir`, `ihm`, `ihr`, `uns`, `euch`, `ihnen`, `Ihnen`; **dative verbs** `gefallen`,
  `gehören`, `helfen`, `gratulieren`.

## Lesson outline
1. `de-a2-u04-l1-invitations` **"Invitations"**: `Ich lade dich ein.` (U3 `dich`), `Wir
   feiern am Samstag.`, `die Einladung`, `die Hochzeit`, `die Gäste`, accepting and
   declining (`Ja, gern!`, `Ich kann leider nicht.`). No dative yet.
2. `de-a2-u04-l2-happy-birthday` **"Happy Birthday!"**: `Herzlichen Glückwunsch zum
   Geburtstag!` (`zum` as part of the chunk), `Alles Gute!`, `Ich gratuliere dir.`, `Das
   Geschenk ist für dich.` vs `Ich schenke dir ein Buch.`, `mir`, `dir`, `Ihnen` (formal).
3. `de-a2-u04-l3-who-gets-what` **"Who Gets What?"**: `Ich schenke meiner Mutter Blumen.`,
   `Wir geben dem Lehrer eine Kerze.`, `einem Freund`, `einer Kollegin`, dative plural
   `den Kindern`, `den Gästen`. Word order: dative noun before accusative noun.
4. `de-a2-u04-l4-i-like-it` **"I Like It"**: `Das Geschenk gefällt mir.`, `Gefällt dir die
   Kerze?`, `Das gehört ihm.`, `Kannst du mir helfen?`, `Ich zeige ihr das Foto.`, `ihnen`.
5. `de-a2-u04-l5-office-birthday` **"Scenario: Cake at the Office"**: a colleague brings a
   cake on their own birthday; the team congratulates and gives a small present: `mitbringen`,
   `die Torte`, `Viel Spaß!`, `die Überraschung`.

## Acceptance criteria
1. Unit `de-a2-u04`, title "Gifts and Celebrations", description "Invite and congratulate
   people, give presents, and say what you like: the dative.", in level A2 after
   `de-a2-u03`, with these lessons in order:
   1. `de-a2-u04-l1-invitations` "Invitations"
   2. `de-a2-u04-l2-happy-birthday` "Happy Birthday!"
   3. `de-a2-u04-l3-who-gets-what` "Who Gets What?"
   4. `de-a2-u04-l4-i-like-it` "I Like It"
   5. `de-a2-u04-l5-office-birthday` "Scenario: Cake at the Office"
2. All of **CDS-1 to CDS-19** pass.
3. Dative pronouns appear in at least 10 exercises across L2–L5; each of `mir`, `dir`,
   `Ihnen`, `ihm`, `ihr`, `ihnen` at least once.
4. Dative articles appear in at least 6 exercises; at least 3 multiple choices have options
   that differ only in the article (`dem / den / der`); the dative plural `-n` appears at
   least twice (`den Kindern`, `den Gästen`).
5. At least 3 word banks put a dative noun before an accusative noun (`Ich schenke meiner
   Schwester ein Buch.`). No sentence has two pronoun objects (`Ich gebe es ihm.` is B1).
6. `gefallen` appears in at least 3 exercises with the thing as the subject; 1 multiple
   choice has the English-style distractor `Ich gefalle das Geschenk.`.
7. `ihnen` and `Ihnen` never share a tile set; dative `ihr` and plural `ihr` never share
   one; prompts mark "(formal)" for `Ihnen` (CDS-17).
8. Celebrations are birthdays, weddings and office or flat parties. No religious holidays
   and no alcohol (PD-051).

Unit total: 33 items. Dative `uns` and `euch` have the same form as the accusative (U3) and
are allowed extras after L4. `dein / sein / ihr / Ihr` take the same dative endings as
`mein` once L3 is done (CDS-16).

## Out of scope
- Dative after prepositions (U5), `passen` / `stehen` (U6)
- Two pronoun objects and their order (B1)
- Religious holidays and alcohol (PD-051)

## UX notes
- Tip examples: "Who gets something? Use the dative: dem Mann, der Frau, den Kindern.";
  "gefallen works the other way round: Das Buch gefällt mir = I like the book."
- L5 is warm and inclusive: invented names, no jokes about age or weight.
